carlgao.com 2026

          case study · design + code
          
This site is my resume, so I built it as a desktop people can poke at: folders, a taskbar, a terminal, and my name in 3D ASCII on the wallpaper. The desktop is one file of plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with no framework and no build step.


          
Type and color

          
Two voices. A monospace for anything the machine says, a serif for anything I say. Amber is the only accent, on a warm near-black.

          

            
AaJetBrains Mono
wallpaper, chrome, terminal

            
AaNewsreader
windows and reading

          

          

            #ffb347 accent
            #0a0a09 desk
            #ece7da text
          


          
Dropping the borrowed chrome

          
The first version dressed up as macOS: blue folders, traffic lights, a glass dock. It looked like a lot of other desktop portfolios, and it fought with the ASCII wallpaper. Now the icons are drawn in the same characters as the name, windows are plain frames, and the dock is a row of text tabs you can also reach with the number keys.


          
The 3D name

          
The name isn't an image or a font effect. A small canvas draws it once, and every filled pixel becomes 3D points on a front face, a back face, and the extruded sides. About 30 times a second those points are rotated and projected onto a grid of characters, and the point nearest the viewer wins each cell. A light drifts in front of the name and picks each character, from . in shadow to @ in full light. Drag the background to spin it.


          
Fixing the Chinese name

          
At ASCII resolution, 高世钦 in a very heavy font turned into a blob. It was worst on phones, where all three characters were squeezed into about 10 rows of text. Thinner strokes, extra space between characters, shallower 3D depth, and stacking 高 over 世钦 on phones made it readable.
